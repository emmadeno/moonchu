It required several minutes of pounding at a great wooden door before any response came; at last some one, I suppose a servant, arrived. Our instructions to our "boy" were about as follows: "Tell the good mandarin we are Americans, come to see Chinese Tea-man-have no place to sleep-will good mandarin let us sleep any place inside his palace?" It should be remembered our "boy" could speak only a little English. We waited long for a reply and the crowd around us at the door was constantly increasing. When the reply came it was not very encouraging. It was: "Mandarin cannot do." Childs, who knew the mandarin character pretty well, said he thought a stronger appeal was necessary-that we must see the mandarin himself; so, to get out of the crowd, by sheer effrontery, we pressed within the outer door; but the crowd followed.
After much wrangling and persuasion by a number of mandarinish flunkies, the crowd was pressed back and the door made fast.
