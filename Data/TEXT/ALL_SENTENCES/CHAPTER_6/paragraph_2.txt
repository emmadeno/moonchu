We are at the northern edge of the river plain, from which a rolling surface extends to the mountains in the distance.
We are looking out toward the great heart of China.
We see a country where only the low land is cultivated and where the hills and mountains are without timber except for an occasional clump of trees.
With us it is usually only the rocky character of the soil which prevents cultivation.
In China there is another cause of neglected cultivation.
It is the vast amount of ground occupied by tombs which can never be removed nor disturbed.
The hills, both to the right and to the left, are old cemeteries.
You can see the partially obliterated graves, but the ground is sacred for all time.
Agriculture and ancestral worship know no truce; these are the state and church in China.
The area of valuable land occupied by graves has long been a serious curtailment of agricultural resources.
This can be better understood when we consider that the venerated graves of ancestry have been preserved for thousands of years.
