The Chinese government is administered through several branches, such as the Board of Civil Office, the Board of Revenue, the Board of Rites, the Board of War, the Board of Punishments, the Board of Works, etc., and only in recent years has a new board been created called the Tsung-li Yamên, or Board of Foreign Affairs.
The whole duty of the Tsung-li Yamên is to receive and confer with foreign ministers and to act as an intermediary between them and the Court.
All diplomatic communications to the different Boards, or to the throne must be made through the Tsung-li Yamên.
No foreign minister can come before the Emperor or the Empress.
This condition of affairs is both inconvenient and offensive.
It was painfully exemplified during the siege of 1900 when this treacherous go-between Board screened the sinister motives of the crafty Empress.
