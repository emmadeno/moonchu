At any rate you have before you three representatives of the notorious Chinese Board of Foreign Affairs.
These are men who were in office during the siege, the one seated is the interpreter.
What do you think of them?
I hope you will not fail to notice the fine feathers in their caps, because I swung two of these dignitaries partially around that you might see them.
These are important men in Chinese affairs; they are members of the newly organized Board.
How we would like to know just what they did and said and thought during those dark days of the siege; being identified with Prince Ching they, of course, pretend that they were friendly, but sincerity is a rare thing in the "Middle Kingdom," and then the faces of Chinamen so often possess a coldness which dispels confidence.
