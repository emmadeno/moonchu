In the center of our field of vision a distant mountain peeps over the shoulder of Victoria Peak.
It is Mount Davis, nearly nine hundred feet high, and around its base is a Chinese cemetery.
Between Mount Davis and the sea, on a gentle slope facing the northeast, thousands of little mounds, designated by simple board tablets, indicate the burial place of the victims of the bubonic plague which has prevailed for many years in this city.
The cemetery is not an attractive resort.
Neither the friends of the victims buried there nor leisure strollers are ever seen near the silent hillside; there even the dead menace the lives of the living.
