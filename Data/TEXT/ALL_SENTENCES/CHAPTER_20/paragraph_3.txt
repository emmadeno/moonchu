You may see two pipes standing on the table by the teacups; they are not opium-pipes, but a not uncommon style of metal tobacco-pipe.
The horizontal bowl is filled with water; the small upright bowl for the tobacco is attached to the top of the water-bowl; a small quantity of tobacco is used at one time; the smoke is drawn through the water and thereby cooled and purified.
Water-pipes in endless varieties are used, not only in China, but in all Oriental countries; so between sipping draughts of mild tea and whiffing a smoke well freed of its harmful alkaloid, the Chinaman whiles away his leisure.
