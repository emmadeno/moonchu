After much wrangling and persuasion by a number of mandarinish flunkies, the crowd was pressed back and the door made fast.
Childs with his gun, accompanied our "boy" into the presence of His Excellency and stated the urgency of our case,—that there was no hotel; that there were plenty bad men outside; that our hong at Hankow "makee" buy plenty tea; these and numerous other necessitous lies induced the mandarin, after a full half-hour of talking, to allow us to sleep in an empty room.
The servants found two rough doors for us to sleep upon.
We were too glad to be within walls to complain of a bed of hard boards, and after a midnight repast we stretched out upon our doors to await the strange disclosures of the coming dawn.
