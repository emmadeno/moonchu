This march of the allied troops between Tien-tsin and Pekin occupied ten days, and they entered the latter place on August 15, just a month and a day after the capture of the former city.
Now that about twenty thousand troops were quartered in the Imperial capital, the transportation of vast quantities of army stores to that place was necessary.
To accomplish this the different armies requisitioned great numbers of native junks, and these plied constantly between Tien-tsin and Tung-Chow, the nearest port to Pekin.
Before boarding one of these junks at Tien-tsin for Tung-Chow I shall tell you briefly how I maintained my existence during and after the investment of the former place.
