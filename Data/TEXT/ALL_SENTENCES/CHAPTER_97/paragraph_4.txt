While the sedan-chair is a very primitive conveyance, every one must confess it is one of the most luxurious, and reminds the rider how well adjusted with springs is the "tailless horse."
When a set of chair-bearers are passing through the narrow streets of a city like Canton they keep up a dismal howl that reminds one of the approach of a band of howling monkeys in the jungles of the Amazon; this notifies the crowds in the street to clear the way, but the warning is so common that it is seldom heeded.
The chair has the right of way, and when people are struck by it they do not seem to mind it; their equanimity can scarcely be disturbed.
In any other country the passage of a sedan-chair would, in a dense thoroughfare, engender endless broils and fisticuffs.
