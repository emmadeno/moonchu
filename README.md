# Master Thesis : Analyzing Text-Image Relationships in 3D Stereoscopic Travel Sets Using Natural Language Processing and Artificial Intelligence

### Abstract

With the increasing presence of Artificial Intelligence in all aspects of life, it is only natural to
question if and how this technology can be applied to the domain of museology. Using a historical
stereoscopic box set as a case study, this thesis explores a wide range of Natural Language Processing
(NLP) and AI-assisted methods in an effort to create a computational framework for transforming
such rich archival collections into immersive, interactive, and narrative museum installations. It
covers all steps of the process, including digital transcription and preprocessing of the archive, and
topic modeling of both images and text. It also addresses the detection of the racist and colonial
language that is typical of such a historical corpus. Finally, the thesis culminates in the description
of a proposed interactive artwork that integrates these computational findings. Throughout all these
steps, it is demonstrated that Large Language Models such as Gemini or ChatGPT show particular
potential in this domain, as they yield very promising results despite the historical nature of the
corpus. Overall, this work provides an open-ended exploration of how these new technologies can
be employed to determine and craft innovative ways of presenting and reviving cultural heritage in
a museological context.
